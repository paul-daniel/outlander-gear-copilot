- Weight: 380 g (size M)

Safety notes:
- Always double-back the waist buckle. Modern buckles auto-lock, but visually verify.
- The belay loop is the rated attachment point for belay devices and carabiners — NEVER clip directly into the tie-in points for belaying.
- Inspect before every climb: check stitching, buckle function, wear on the belay loop and tie-in points. If the belay loop shows core (white) fibers, retire the harness immediately.

DYNAMIC ROPE 70 M (€189.00)
------------------------------
A workhorse single rope for sport, trad, and multi-pitch climbing. The 9.8 mm diameter balances durability with handling, and the dry-treated sheath sheds moisture in damp conditions.

Specifications:
- Type: Single rope
- Diameter: 9.8 mm
- Length: 70 m
- Treatment: Dry-treated sheath
- Falls held (UIAA): 7+
- Impact force: 8.4 kN
- Static elongation: 8.2%
- Dynamic elongation: 32%
- Weight: 62 g/m (4.20 kg total)
- Sheath slippage: 0 mm
- Certification: EN 892, UIAA

Why 70 meters? Modern sport routes are often bolted for 35 m pitches. A 70 m rope lets you lower from any 35 m pitch and still reach the ground. Always tie a knot in the free end of your rope when lowering.

Rope care:
- Store loosely coiled or in a rope bag — never hang it from a single point for extended periods.
- Flake the rope before each climb to remove twists.
- Wash in lukewarm water with mild soap; air-dry in shade.
- Retire after: a significant fall on a sharp edge, visible core damage, 5 years of regular weekend use, or 10 years regardless of use.

When to wear a helmet:
- Sport climbing outdoors (ALWAYS — rockfall can happen on any crag)
- Trad and multi-pitch climbing

- Ice climbing

You can skip a helmet only in a fully controlled indoor gym environment where there is no rockfall risk.

CLIMBING SAFETY CHECKLIST
---------------------------
Before every climb, perform the buddy check:
1. HARNESS: Waist buckle doubled back, leg loops secure, belay loop intact.
2. KNOT: Figure-eight follow-through tied correctly and dressed. Tail at least 15 cm long.
3. CARABINER: Belay carabiner locked. Gate facing opposite direction of potential load.
4. BELAY DEVICE: Rope loaded correctly (bight through device, clipped to belay loop).
5. HELMET: On, chinstrap fastened.
6. ROPE: Knot in the free end when sport climbing outdoors.

FALL FACTORS EXPLAINED
-----------------------
Fall Factor = Fall Distance ÷ Rope Length Out

- Fall Factor 0: You fall with slack below you but the rope catches immediately (e.g., top-rope). Low force.
- Fall Factor 1: You fall the distance of rope between you and the anchor (e.g., falling from anchor level). Moderate force.
- Fall Factor 2: You fall twice the length of rope out (e.g., leading above the belay with no intermediate clips). Maximum force on a single rope — this is the worst-case scenario.

Our Dynamic Rope 70 m is tested to survive 7+ factor-2 falls in lab conditions. In real climbing, ropes rarely experience factor-2 falls due to intermediate protection points.
