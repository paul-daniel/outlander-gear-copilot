OUTLANDER GEAR CO. — MATERIALS AND TECHNOLOGY GUIDE
=====================================================

Understanding the materials and technologies in outdoor gear helps you make informed buying decisions. This guide explains what goes into our products and why.

FABRIC TECHNOLOGIES
--------------------

Waterproof / Breathable Membranes
Our StormShield Waterproof Jacket (€259.00) uses a 3-layer construction:
- Outer face fabric: Durable nylon with DWR (Durable Water Repellent) finish
- Membrane: ePTFE (expanded polytetrafluoroethylene) — the same family of membranes as Gore-Tex
- Inner liner: Knit backer protects the membrane from body oils and abrasion

Key metrics:
- Waterproofness: 20,000 mm hydrostatic head (anything above 10,000 mm handles sustained heavy rain)
- Breathability: 20,000 g/m²/24 h MVTR (moisture vapor transmission rate)
- Seam sealing: Fully taped seams prevent water entry through needle holes

What the numbers mean: A 20 k/20 k (waterproof/breathability) rating is high-end performance. For reference: light rain = 2,000 mm is sufficient; heavy sustained rain with a pack = 15,000+ mm recommended.

DWR (Durable Water Repellent)
DWR is a chemical coating applied to the outer fabric that makes water bead and roll off rather than saturating the face fabric. When DWR wears off:
- Water soaks into (but not through) the membrane — the jacket still keeps rain out, but the wet face fabric reduces breathability and adds weight.
- Restore DWR by washing the jacket and tumble drying on low heat, or spray on a DWR treatment.

Ripstop Nylon
Used in our Alpine Pro Tent 2P (€349.99), Day Hike Pack 28L (€89.95), and Trail Running Vest 12L (€119.00). Ripstop is a weaving technique where reinforcement threads are woven in a grid pattern to prevent small tears from spreading. Lighter and stronger than standard nylon at equivalent thicknesses.

INSULATION TYPES
-----------------

Down (Natural)
Our Down Jacket 700 Fill (€179.00) uses 700-cuin RDS (Responsible Down Standard) duck down.
- Fill power: 700 cubic inches per ounce — this measures loft (how much air the down traps). Higher = warmer for its weight.
- RDS certified: Ensures humane treatment of birds.
- Pros: Best warmth-to-weight ratio of any insulation, compresses to tiny volume, lasts many years.
- Cons: Loses insulating ability when wet.
- Care: Machine wash on gentle cycle with down-specific detergent. Tumble dry low with 2–3 clean tennis balls to restore loft.

Our Expedition Sleeping Bag −15 °C (€299.50) uses 800-cuin RDS goose down — even higher loft for extreme cold.

Synthetic (PrimaLoft)
Our Alpinist Boots (€389.00) use PrimaLoft 200 g insulation in the boot liner.
- Pros: Retains warmth when wet, dries quickly, hypoallergenic.
- Cons: Heavier and bulkier than down for equivalent warmth.
- Why in boots: Boots inevitably get wet from sweat, puddles, and snow. Synthetic insulation keeps working even when damp.

Merino Wool
Our Merino Base Layer Top (€79.00) uses 185 g/m² Merino wool.
- Fiber fineness: 18.5 micron (does not itch)
- Temperature regulation: Wool naturally insulates when cold and breathes when warm.
- Odor resistance: Wool's lanolin and fiber structure resist bacterial growth — you can wear it for multiple days before it smells.
- Moisture management: Absorbs up to 30% of its weight in moisture before feeling wet.
- Care: Machine wash cold on wool cycle. Lay flat to dry.

TENT FABRICS AND COATINGS
---------------------------
Our Alpine Pro Tent 2P (€349.99):
- Fly: 20D ripstop nylon, silicone/PU double-coated (3,000 mm HH)
- Floor: 30D ripstop nylon, PU-coated (5,000 mm HH) — higher rating because the floor contacts wet ground and you kneel/sit on it.
- Poles: 7001 T6 aluminum alloy — light, strong, and springy. Resists permanent deformation in wind.
- Mesh: Inner tent has full mesh panels for ventilation, keeping condensation in the vestibule rather than inside the sleeping area.

Silicone vs. PU coating:
- Silicone: Lighter, more UV resistant, fabric remains supple. Cannot be seam-taped (requires seam sealing with liquid silicone sealant).
- PU (polyurethane): Heavier, can be seam-taped in the factory for guaranteed waterproofness. Degrades faster with UV exposure.
- Our tent fly uses a silicone/PU hybrid: silicone outside for UV resistance and weight savings, PU inside for factory seam taping.

SOLE TECHNOLOGIES
------------------
Most of our footwear uses Vibram outsoles:
- Trail Grip X (€159.99): Vibram Megagrip — sticky rubber compound for wet rock and dry trail grip.
- Mid Hiking Boots (€169.00): Vibram outsole with 5 mm lugs — deep tread for loose terrain.

Midsole materials:
- EVA (ethylene-vinyl acetate): Lightweight, good cushioning, used in Trail Grip X. Compresses over time (~800 km lifespan).
- PU (polyurethane): Denser, more durable, used in Mid Hiking Boots. Lasts longer under heavy loads but weighs more.
- TPU shank: A rigid thermoplastic plate in the Alpinist Boots that enables crampon compatibility and prevents flex on rough terrain.

STOVE TECHNOLOGY
-----------------
Our GasPro Compact Stove (€59.95):
- Fuel: Isobutane/propane canister (screw-on, EN 417)
- Boil time: 3.5 min per 1 L water
- Burn time: 60 min on 230 g canister
- Piezo ignition: Built-in (no matches needed)
- Weight: 95 g (stove head only)

Canister stoves vs. liquid fuel: Canister stoves are lighter, simpler, and cleaner-burning. They lose pressure in temperatures below −10 °C. For extreme cold, use a canister inverter or switch to liquid fuel. Our GasPro performs well down to about −5 °C.
