Outlander Gear Co. — Materials and Technology Guide

Understanding the materials and technologies used in outdoor gear helps you make informed purchasing decisions and get the most performance from your equipment. This guide explains every key technology and material referenced in our product catalog.

Part 1: Waterproofing Technologies

Hydrostatic Head (Waterproof Rating):
Measured in millimeters (mm), this is the pressure a fabric can withstand before water penetrates. It is tested by placing fabric under a column of water and measuring when the first drop comes through.
- 1,500mm: Light rain protection. Minimum for a waterproof garment.
- 2,000mm: Moderate rain. Suitable for casual outdoor jackets.
- 3,000mm: Heavy rain. Our Alpine Pro tent fly is rated at 3,000mm — it will keep you dry in sustained heavy downpours.
- 5,000mm: Very heavy rain and ground moisture pressure. Our Alpine Pro tent floor is rated at 5,000mm because the floor must withstand the additional pressure of your body weight and gear pressing down on wet ground.
- 10,000–20,000mm: Premium waterproof jackets. This is the range where high-performance hardshells operate.
- 20,000mm+: The maximum waterproofing for technical mountaineering and extreme conditions.

DWR (Durable Water Repellent):
DWR is a coating applied to the outer surface of waterproof fabrics. It causes water to bead up and roll off instead of soaking into the fabric face. Without DWR, water saturates the outer fabric layer (called "wetting out"), which blocks the breathable membrane's ability to function — making the garment feel clammy and heavy even though it is not leaking.

DWR wears off over time from abrasion, dirt, body oils, and washing. It must be periodically reapplied using spray-on or wash-in treatments (such as Nikwax TX.Direct). The first sign that DWR needs refreshing is when water no longer beads on the surface and instead darkens the fabric.

Gore-Tex:
Gore-Tex is the most recognized waterproof-breathable membrane in the outdoor industry. It works through a thin PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) membrane with billions of microscopic pores. These pores are large enough to allow water vapor (sweat) to escape but too small for liquid water droplets to penetrate.

Our Alpinist Mountaineering Boots use Gore-Tex Insulated — a Gore-Tex waterproof membrane combined with insulation, providing both waterproofing and warmth in a single construction.

Gore-Tex ratings in context:
- Gore-Tex standard: Suitable for general outdoor use
- Gore-Tex Active: Lighter, more breathable for high-output activities
- Gore-Tex Pro: Maximum durability and protection for extreme conditions
- Gore-Tex Insulated: Combined waterproofing and insulation for cold environments

Sealed Seams:
Waterproof fabrics are only waterproof if the seams are also sealed. Needle holes from stitching create pathways for water. Seam sealing involves applying waterproof tape over every seam on the inside of the garment. Our StormShield jacket has fully sealed seams throughout — no half measures.

Part 2: Breathability

Breathability is measured in grams per square meter per 24 hours (g/m²/24h), indicating how much moisture vapor the fabric can transmit. Higher numbers mean more breathable:
- 5,000 g/m²/24h: Low breathability. Acceptable for low-output activities in mild conditions.
- 10,000 g/m²/24h: Moderate. Suitable for hiking and general use.
- 15,000 g/m²/24h: Good. Comfortable for sustained moderate activity.
- 20,000 g/m²/24h: Excellent. Our StormShield jacket achieves 20,000 g/m²/24h — among the best in the industry. This means you can hike strenuously in the rain without the jacket feeling clammy from trapped sweat.
- 25,000+ g/m²/24h: Ultra-breathable. Typically found in Gore-Tex Active or similar competition membranes.

The balance between waterproofing and breathability is the fundamental challenge in outdoor clothing design. Generally, higher waterproofing reduces breathability and vice versa. Our StormShield achieves both high waterproofing and high breathability through its 3-layer construction — a major engineering achievement.

3-Layer vs. 2-Layer vs. 2.5-Layer Construction:
- 3-Layer (like our StormShield): The waterproof membrane is laminated between an outer face fabric and an inner protective lining, creating a single unified fabric. Most durable and breathable, but also the heaviest and most expensive.
- 2.5-Layer: The membrane is bonded to an outer face fabric with a thin printed pattern (the "half layer") protecting the membrane inside. Lighter and more packable than 3-layer but less durable.
- 2-Layer: The membrane is bonded only to the outer fabric. Requires a separate hanging mesh liner to protect the membrane. Heaviest option and the least clammy-feeling due to the air gap.

Part 3: Insulation Technologies

Down Fill Power (Cuin):
Fill power measures the quality (loft) of down insulation. It is expressed in cubic inches per ounce (cuin) — the volume that one ounce of down occupies when allowed to fully expand:
- 550–600 cuin: Budget down. Heavier for the same warmth.
- 650–700 cuin: Good quality. Our Doudoune jacket uses 700 cuin down — an excellent balance of warmth, weight, and cost.
- 750–800 cuin: Premium. Our Expedition Sleeping Bag uses 800 cuin goose down — the pinnacle of natural insulation.
- 850–900+ cuin: Ultra-premium. The lightest and most compressible, but also the most expensive.

Higher fill power means the same weight of down traps more air (and therefore more warmth). An 800 cuin sleeping bag achieves the same warmth as a 650 cuin bag while being significantly lighter and more compressible.

Goose Down vs. Duck Down:
Goose down clusters are generally larger than duck down clusters, which is why the highest fill powers (800+) are typically goose down. Our Expedition bag uses goose down for maximum performance. At 700 cuin and below, the difference between goose and duck is minimal.

RDS Certification (Responsible Down Standard):
Our Doudoune 700 cuin jacket uses RDS-certified down. The Responsible Down Standard ensures that down feathers are sourced from ducks and geese that have been treated humanely — no live-plucking, no force-feeding. This certification is verified through third-party audits of every step in the supply chain.

Thinsulate Insulation:
A synthetic insulation made by 3M using ultra-fine microfibers that trap air efficiently. Our Alpinist boots use 400g Thinsulate insulation:
- The "400g" refers to the weight of insulation per square meter (400 grams).
- Thinsulate retains insulating ability when wet (unlike down).
- It is thin relative to its warmth — hence the name — making it ideal for footwear where bulk is a problem.
- Temperature rating: 400g Thinsulate is suitable for extended periods in temperatures well below -20°C depending on activity level.

R-Value (Sleeping Pads):
R-value measures thermal resistance — the ability of a sleeping pad to resist heat flow from your body to the cold ground. It is an absolute number (not relative) and was standardized across the industry in 2020 using the ASTM F3340-18 test method.
- R 1.0–2.0: Summer only. Ground temperatures above 10°C.
- R 2.5–3.5: 3-season. Ground temperatures above 0°C.
- R 4.0–5.5: 4-season. Ground temperatures below freezing. Our UltraLight pad (R 4.2) is in this range.
- R 5.5+: Winter expedition. Frozen ground, snow camping.

R-values are additive. If you stack our R 4.2 UltraLight pad on top of a closed-cell foam pad (R 2.0), you get a combined R 6.2 — suitable for winter camping.

Part 4: Outsole Technologies

Vibram:
Vibram is an Italian company founded in 1937, and the world leader in high-performance outsoles. Their rubber compounds and tread patterns are used on premium hiking boots, trail runners, and mountaineering footwear.

Vibram Megagrip (used on our Trail Grip X):
- Maximum traction on both wet and dry surfaces.
- Superior durability — resists wear over hundreds of kilometers.
- Multidirectional lug pattern for confidence on varied terrain.
- One of the stickiest rubber compounds Vibram produces — it grips wet rock almost as well as dry.

Crampon Compatibility Ratings:
- C1: Flexible sole. Compatible with strap-on crampons only. Typical of hiking boots.
- C2: Semi-rigid sole with a rear welt. Compatible with semi-automatic crampons. Typical of stiff hiking and approach boots.
- C3: Rigid sole with front and rear welts. Compatible with fully automatic (step-in) crampons. Our Alpinist boots are C3-rated — the highest level, required for technical ice climbing and steep alpine terrain.

Part 5: Fabric Technologies

UV Protection (UPF):
UPF (Ultraviolet Protection Factor) rates how much UV radiation a fabric blocks:
- UPF 15–24: Good protection (blocks 93.3–95.9% of UV).
- UPF 25–39: Very good protection (blocks 96.0–97.4% of UV).
- UPF 40–50+: Excellent protection (blocks 97.5–98%+ of UV). Our Convertible Trek Pants are rated UPF 50+ — the highest possible rating.

Unlike sunscreen (which must be reapplied), UPF protection in fabric is permanent and does not degrade with washing or use.

Stretch Fabrics:
Our Convertible Trek Pants use a stretch fabric that allows freedom of movement during hiking, climbing, and scrambling. Stretch is typically achieved through elastane (Spandex/Lycra) blended with nylon or polyester. The percentage of elastane determines the degree of stretch — typically 3–8% for hiking pants.

Part 6: Pole and Frame Technologies

DAC Aluminum:
DAC (Dongah Aluminum Corporation) is the world's premier manufacturer of tent poles. Based in South Korea, DAC supplies poles to most major tent brands. Our Alpine Pro tent uses DAC aluminum poles because:
- Exceptional strength-to-weight ratio
- Flexibility that prevents snapping (poles flex in wind rather than breaking)
- Pre-bent sections for optimal tent geometry
- Anodized finish for corrosion resistance

Carbon Fiber (Trekking Poles):
Our Carbon Ultra Trekking Poles use carbon fiber for:
- Maximum weight savings (380g per pair is exceptionally light)
- Excellent vibration dampening — carbon absorbs trail vibrations better than aluminum, reducing fatigue on long days
- High stiffness-to-weight ratio

The tradeoff: carbon can snap under extreme lateral force (stepping on a pole), while aluminum bends and can be field-repaired.

FlickLock Adjustment:
Our trekking poles use the FlickLock system — external lever-lock mechanisms that clamp the telescoping pole sections:
- Easy to adjust with gloves on
- Strong holding power — will not slip under load
- No tools needed for adjustment
- Works reliably in wet, muddy, and icy conditions
- Quick to deploy and collapse

Part 7: Stove Technologies

Piezo Igniter:
An electronic ignition system that creates a spark when a button is pressed, eliminating the need for matches or a lighter. Our Compact GasPro has an integrated piezo igniter. Note: piezo igniters become less reliable at high altitude (lower air pressure) and in cold temperatures. Always carry a backup lighter.

Canister Fuel (Isobutane/Propane):
Our GasPro stove uses standard threaded (EN 417) gas canisters containing an isobutane-propane blend:
- Self-pressurized: no pumping required
- Clean-burning: no soot or priming
- Precise flame control
- Efficient in moderate temperatures (above 5°C)
- Performance decreases in cold weather as canister pressure drops. Warm the canister in your sleeping bag before use in cold conditions.

Power Output (Watts):
Our GasPro produces 2,800W of thermal output. For reference:
- 1,500W: Basic ultralight stove. Slow boil times.
- 2,500–3,000W: Standard backpacking stove. Boils 1L in 3–4 minutes. Our GasPro sits in this sweet spot.
- 4,000W+: High-output stove for group cooking or melting snow.

Part 8: Hydration Technologies

18/8 Stainless Steel:
Our insulated water bottle uses 18/8 stainless steel (also called 304 stainless steel). The numbers refer to the composition: 18% chromium and 8% nickel.
- Chromium provides corrosion resistance.
- Nickel adds strength and a polished finish.
- 18/8 is food-safe, BPA-free, and does not impart flavors.
- It is heavier than aluminum or plastic but far more durable and long-lasting.

Double-Wall Vacuum Insulation:
Our bottle uses two walls of stainless steel with a vacuum between them. Because there is no air in the vacuum, there are no air molecules to conduct heat — this is the most effective insulation possible. Our bottle keeps drinks cold for 24 hours and hot for 12 hours under standard conditions.

Hollow Fiber Membrane Filtration:
Our MiniPure water filter uses hollow fiber membrane technology:
- Thousands of microscopic hollow tubes (fibers) with pore sizes of 0.1 micron (0.0001mm).
- Water is forced through the walls of these fibers. Bacteria (typically 0.2–5 microns) and protozoa (typically 1–15 microns) are physically too large to pass through.
- Removes 99.9999% of bacteria (including E. coli, Salmonella, Cholera) and 99.9% of protozoa (including Giardia, Cryptosporidium).
- Does NOT remove viruses (which are 0.02–0.3 microns) — in regions where waterborne viruses are a concern, use chemical treatment (tablets or UV) in addition to filtering.
- Does NOT remove chemical contaminants, heavy metals, or dissolved minerals.
- Flow rate: 1 liter per minute — fast enough to fill a bottle in real time.
- Lifespan: 4,000 liters before the filter element must be replaced.

Part 9: IPX Water Resistance Ratings

Our headlamp is rated IPX6. Here is what the IPX scale means:
- IPX0: No protection.
- IPX1: Protection against vertical dripping water.
- IPX2: Protection against dripping water at 15° tilt.
- IPX3: Protection against spraying water.
- IPX4: Protection against splashing water from any direction.
- IPX5: Protection against water jets.
- IPX6: Protection against powerful water jets. Our 600-lumen headlamp is rated IPX6 — it will survive heavy rain, river crossings, and direct splashing.
- IPX7: Protection against temporary submersion (up to 1m for 30 minutes).
- IPX8: Protection against continuous submersion (manufacturer-specified depth).

Our headlamp's IPX6 rating means you can wear it confidently in the heaviest downpours and even during canyoneering or rafting, but it should not be submerged.
