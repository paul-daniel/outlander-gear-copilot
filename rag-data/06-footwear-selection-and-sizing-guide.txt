Part 2: How to Choose the Right Footwear

Decision Factors:

1. Activity Type:
- Day hiking on maintained trails → Trail runners or light hiking boots
- Multi-day backpacking with 15–25 kg pack → Mid-cut hiking boots
- Technical scrambling or via ferrata → Approach shoes or stiff hiking boots
- Alpine climbing / glacier travel → Mountaineering boots (Alpinist)
- Trail running / ultralight fast packing → Trail runners (Trail Grip X)

2. Terrain:
- Smooth dirt trails → Almost anything works; trail runners are most enjoyable.
- Rocky, uneven terrain → A stiffer sole and more robust construction protect your feet from bruising on sharp rocks. Hiking boots or stiff trail runners.
- Snow, ice, and glaciers → Mountaineering boots with crampon compatibility (Alpinist).
- Wet, muddy trails → Waterproof membrane (Gore-Tex or equivalent) is highly recommended. Both our Trail Grip X and Alpinist boots are waterproof.

3. Pack Weight:
The heavier your pack, the more support and stiffness you need from your footwear. Carrying 25+ kg in trail runners on rough terrain is a recipe for rolled ankles and sore feet. Match your shoes to your load.

4. Personal Preference:
Some hikers swear by the freedom and speed of trail runners even for multi-day treks with moderate loads. Others prefer the security and protection of boots. There is no single right answer — what matters is that your shoes fit well and match your terrain and load.

Part 3: Sizing Guide

Getting the right size is critical for outdoor footwear. Shoes that are too small cause blisters, blackened toenails, and pain on descents. Shoes that are too large cause sliding, instability, and blisters from friction.

General Sizing Rules:
1. Order a half-size to full size larger than your street shoe size for hiking footwear. Your feet swell during long hikes, and you need clearance in the toe box for downhill sections.
2. Try shoes on in the afternoon or evening, when your feet are at their largest (feet swell during the day).
3. Wear the socks you plan to hike in when trying on shoes. Thick hiking socks take up more volume than dress socks.

Width:
Most hiking shoes come in a standard width. If you have wide feet, look for models labeled "Wide" or choose brands known for generous toe boxes. If shoes feel tight across the forefoot (ball of the foot), you may need a wider size rather than a longer one.

How to Measure Your Feet at Home:
1. Place a piece of paper on a hard floor against a wall.
2. Stand on the paper with your heel touching the wall.
3. Mark the furthest point of your longest toe.
4. Measure the distance from the wall to the mark in centimeters.
5. Add 1.0–1.5 cm for hiking shoes (to account for swelling and downhill clearance).
6. Use the size chart to find your EU or US size.

Part 4: Breaking In Your Footwear

Modern hiking boots require less break-in than leather boots of the past, but some break-in is still recommended:
1. Wear new boots around the house for several hours over 2–3 days.
2. Take short walks (1–2 km) on paved surfaces, gradually increasing distance.
3. Do a short hike (3–5 km) with a light pack before committing to a multi-day trek.
4. Pay attention to hot spots (areas of friction or pressure). Address these with moleskin, different lacing techniques, or different socks before they become blisters.

Trail runners (like our Trail Grip X) typically require little to no break-in time — they are designed to be comfortable right out of the box.

Part 6: Sock Recommendations

The right socks are just as important as the right shoes:
- Material: Merino wool is the gold standard for hiking socks. It wicks moisture, regulates temperature, and resists odor naturally. Synthetic blends (nylon/polyester with merino) add durability.
- Cushioning: Match cushion to activity — thin socks for trail running, medium cushion for day hikes, heavy cushion for cold weather and heavy packs.
- Height: Crew-length or higher for boots (to prevent rubbing from the boot collar). Ankle-length for trail runners.
- Avoid cotton: Cotton absorbs moisture and does not dry, leading to blisters and cold feet.
