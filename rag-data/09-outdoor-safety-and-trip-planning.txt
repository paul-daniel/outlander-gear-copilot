OUTLANDER GEAR CO. — OUTDOOR SAFETY AND TRIP PLANNING
=======================================================

Preparation is the difference between an adventure and an emergency. This guide covers essential safety practices, trip planning, and the gear that keeps you safe.

THE TEN ESSENTIALS
--------------------
Every outdoor trip — even a short day hike — should include these items:

1. Navigation: Map + compass or GPS device. Know how to use them.
2. Sun protection: Sunglasses, sunscreen, hat.
3. Insulation: Extra clothing layers beyond what you expect to need. Bring our StormShield Waterproof Jacket (€259.00) even on clear days — mountain weather changes fast.
4. Illumination: Headlamp with extra batteries. Our Headlamp 600 Lumens (€49.99) has 60 h runtime on low mode.
5. First-aid supplies: Bandages, antiseptic, blister pads, personal medications.
6. Fire: Waterproof matches or lighter + fire-starter.
7. Repair tools: Knife, duct tape, cord.
8. Nutrition: Extra food (one meal beyond planned).
9. Hydration: Extra water + purification method. Our MiniPure Water Filter (€44.95) filters to 0.1 micron.
10. Emergency shelter: Bivy sack or emergency blanket.

WEATHER AWARENESS
------------------
Before every trip:
- Check the mountain weather forecast (not just the valley forecast).
- Know the freezing level and wind speeds at altitude.
- Identify bail-out points on your route in case weather deteriorates.
- Start early: In summer mountains, afternoon thunderstorms are common. Aim to be below ridgelines by 1:00 PM.

Lightning safety:
- If thunder is less than 30 seconds after lightning, seek shelter immediately.
- Avoid ridgelines, isolated trees, bodies of water, and metal objects.
- If caught in the open, crouch low on the balls of your feet with feet together. Do not lie flat.

WATER SAFETY
--------------
Never drink untreated water from streams, lakes, or rivers — even in pristine-looking mountain environments. Common waterborne threats:
- Giardia lamblia (protozoan): Causes severe diarrhea 1–2 weeks after ingestion.
- Cryptosporidium (protozoan): Similar symptoms, resistant to chemical treatment.
- E. coli and Salmonella (bacteria): Contamination from animal waste upstream.

NAVIGATION BASICS
-------------------
- Always carry a paper map as backup, even if you use a GPS device.
- Before your trip, study the map: identify landmarks, water sources, and escape routes.
- On the trail, check your map every 30 minutes to stay oriented.
- In poor visibility, use compass bearings between known points.
- GPS batteries die; phone screens break. Never rely solely on electronics.

ALTITUDE SAFETY
-----------------
Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) can affect anyone above 2,500 m, regardless of fitness level.

Symptoms: Headache, nausea, dizziness, fatigue, insomnia.

Prevention:
- Ascend no more than 300–500 m per day above 2,500 m.
- "Climb high, sleep low" — ascend to a higher point during the day, but descend to sleep.
- Stay well hydrated (3–4 L per day). Our Hydration Bladder 3 L (€29.95) and Insulated Bottle 1 L (€32.95) make it easy to carry enough water.
- Avoid alcohol in the first 48 h at altitude.

Treatment:
- Mild AMS: Rest at current altitude, hydrate, take ibuprofen for headache.
- Moderate to severe AMS: DESCEND IMMEDIATELY. Do not wait to "see if it gets better."

TRIP PLANNING CHECKLIST
-------------------------
1. Route research: Distance, elevation gain, terrain type, water sources, shelter options.
2. Permits: Check if your destination requires hiking, camping, or fire permits.
3. Share your plan: Tell someone your route, expected return time, and emergency plan.
4. Gear check: Lay out all gear the night before. Use our packing loadouts in the Hiking and Trekking Gear Guide.
5. Food planning: Calculate 2,500–3,500 calories per day for active hiking. Pack lightweight, calorie-dense foods.
6. Water planning: Identify natural water sources on your route and plan filtration/carry capacity accordingly.

EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS
---------------------
If something goes wrong:
1. STOP: Sit down, stay calm, assess the situation.
2. THINK: What resources do you have? What is the safest option?
3. OBSERVE: Check your surroundings for shelter, water, landmarks.
4. PLAN: Make a plan and stick to it.

Signaling for help:
- International distress signal: 6 blasts of a whistle (or 6 flashes of a light) per minute, then pause for 1 minute and repeat.
- In Europe: Call 112 from any phone (works even without a SIM card).
- If no signal: Stay put, make yourself visible, and wait for search and rescue.

LEAVE NO TRACE PRINCIPLES
----------------------------
1. Plan ahead and prepare.
2. Travel and camp on durable surfaces.
3. Dispose of waste properly (pack out all trash; bury human waste 15 cm deep, 60 m from water).
4. Leave what you find.
5. Minimize campfire impacts (use a stove instead when possible — our GasPro Compact Stove leaves zero trace).
6. Respect wildlife.
7. Be considerate of other visitors.
