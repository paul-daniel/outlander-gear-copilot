Outlander Gear Co. — Product Care and Maintenance Guide

Proper care and maintenance of your outdoor gear extends its lifespan, maintains performance, and ensures your safety in the field. This comprehensive guide covers care instructions for every product category available at Outlander Gear Co.

Section 1: Tent Care and Maintenance

Setting Up and Taking Down Your Tent:
Always clear the ground area of sharp rocks, sticks, and debris before pitching your tent. Use a footprint or groundsheet underneath the tent floor to provide an extra layer of protection against abrasion and moisture. When packing up, shake out all dirt, leaves, and debris from the interior and fly before folding.

Cleaning Your Tent:
- After each trip, set up the tent at home and allow it to air dry completely before storing. This is the single most important step in tent care — storing a damp tent leads to mold, mildew, and degraded waterproof coatings.
- For light cleaning, use a damp sponge with lukewarm water to wipe down the fabric.
- For deeper cleaning, use a specialized tent cleaner (such as Nikwax Tech Wash). Never use standard laundry detergent, bleach, or fabric softener, as these chemicals damage the waterproof coatings and DWR (Durable Water Repellent) treatments.
- Never machine-wash or machine-dry your tent. The agitation and heat will destroy the fabric coatings and stress the seams.

Maintaining Waterproofing:
Over time, the factory DWR coating on your tent fly and floor will wear off. You can restore it by applying a DWR reproofing spray (such as Nikwax TX.Direct) after cleaning. For seam sealing, check seams annually and apply seam sealer to any areas that show signs of delamination or leakage.

Storage:
Store your tent loosely packed in a large breathable cotton or mesh bag — not in the tight stuff sack. This prevents the fabric and coatings from being compressed for extended periods, which can cause permanent creasing and coating damage. Store in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight (UV degrades nylon and polyester over time).

Our Alpine Pro 3-Season Tent uses DAC aluminum poles and a 3000mm waterproof fly with 5000mm reinforced floor. With proper care, this tent will provide many years of reliable shelter.

Section 2: Sleeping Bag Care

Washing a Down Sleeping Bag:
Down sleeping bags (like our Expedition Sleeping Bag -15°C with 800-fill goose down) require special care. They should be washed infrequently — only when they become noticeably dirty or lose loft.
- Use a front-loading washing machine (never top-loading with an agitator).
- Wash on a gentle cycle with cold or lukewarm water.
- Use a down-specific detergent (such as Nikwax Down Wash Direct or Grangers Down Wash). Standard detergents strip the natural oils from down clusters, reducing their insulating ability.
- Rinse twice to ensure all detergent is removed.
- Tumble dry on low heat with 2–3 clean tennis balls. The tennis balls help break up clumps of wet down and restore loft. Drying may take 2–4 hours — be patient and ensure the bag is completely dry before storage.

Storage:
Never store a down sleeping bag compressed in its stuff sack for extended periods. Prolonged compression crushes the down clusters and permanently reduces loft (and insulating performance). Store your sleeping bag in the large storage sack provided or hang it in a dry closet. Our Expedition -15°C bag should maintain its 800 cuin fill power for many years with proper storage.

Section 3: Sleeping Pad Care

Caring for the UltraLight Inflatable Sleeping Pad:
- After each use, inflate the pad and wipe it down with a damp cloth to remove dirt and sweat.
- Allow it to dry completely before rolling and packing. Store it unrolled and partially inflated with the valve open to prevent the foam core from permanently compressing.
- Avoid placing the pad directly on rough surfaces without a tent floor or footprint underneath.
- Carry a repair kit (patch kit is included) for field repairs of small punctures.
- Our UltraLight pad has an R-value of 4.2, providing excellent insulation from cold ground. Maintaining the integrity of the air chambers is essential for this insulation to work.

Section 4: Stove Care and Maintenance

Compact GasPro Stove:
- After each use, let the stove cool completely before handling.
- Wipe the burner head and pot support with a damp cloth to remove food residue.
- Inspect the piezo igniter regularly and replace the igniter element if it fails to spark.
- Check the gas valve and fuel line for debris. Use a thin needle to clear any blocked burner ports.
- Store the stove in its protective case and keep it dry. Avoid storing with a gas canister attached.
- Before each trip, do a test burn at home to ensure everything functions properly.

Section 5: Clothing Care

Hardshell Jackets (StormShield Waterproof Jacket):
- Machine wash on a gentle cycle with cold water using a technical fabric wash (Nikwax Tech Wash). Do not use regular detergent or fabric softener.
- After washing, tumble dry on low heat for 20 minutes. The heat reactivates the DWR (Durable Water Repellent) coating. Alternatively, use a warm iron on a low setting with a cloth barrier.
- If water no longer beads on the surface after washing and reactivating, apply a DWR reproofing treatment (Nikwax TX.Direct spray-on or wash-in).
- Hang dry in a well-ventilated area. Avoid direct sunlight for prolonged periods.
- Our StormShield jacket features 3-layer construction with sealed seams and 20,000 g/m²/24h breathability. Proper care maintains both waterproofing and breathability.

Down Jackets (Doudoune 700 Cuin):
- Follow the same washing procedure as down sleeping bags (front-loader, down-specific detergent, tumble dry with tennis balls).
- Spot clean minor stains with a damp cloth and mild soap rather than washing the entire jacket.
- Store hanging or loosely folded — not compressed in the stuff pocket for long periods.

Convertible Trek Pants:
- Machine wash on a gentle cycle with cold water.
- The DWR finish can be reactivated by tumble drying on low heat.
- The stretch fabric has UV50+ protection that remains effective throughout the garment's lifespan.
- Zip the leg sections back on before washing to protect the zippers.

Section 6: Footwear Care

Trail Shoes and Hiking Boots:
- Remove insoles and loosen laces after each use to allow shoes to air out completely.
- Brush off dried mud with a stiff brush. For stubborn dirt, use lukewarm water and a soft brush. Avoid submerging shoes in water.
- Never machine wash or machine dry hiking shoes. Heat from dryers destroys adhesives and deforms shoe structures.
- Apply a waterproofing treatment appropriate for the shoe material (spray-on for synthetic membranes like Gore-Tex, wax-based for leather).
- Store shoes in a cool, dry place with shoe trees or crumpled newspaper inside to maintain shape and absorb moisture.
- Our Trail Grip X shoes feature Vibram Megagrip outsoles — keep the lugs clean for optimal traction. Our Alpinist mountaineering boots use Gore-Tex Insulated lining and Thinsulate 400g insulation; ensure they are completely dry before storage to prevent mold.

Recovery Sandals:
- Rinse with clean water after each use. The EVA material is quick-drying and low-maintenance.
- Avoid leaving sandals in direct sunlight for extended periods, as UV exposure can cause the EVA to become brittle over time.

Section 7: Accessories Care

Headlamp (600 Lumens):
- Remove the battery before long-term storage to prevent corrosion.
- Wipe the lens with a soft microfiber cloth. Avoid abrasive materials that could scratch the lens.
- The IPX6 water resistance means it can withstand heavy rain and splashing, but it is not designed for submersion. Rinse with fresh water after exposure to saltwater or muddy conditions.
- Recharge via USB-C before each trip and carry a backup power source for extended adventures.

Multi-Function Knife:
- Rinse the knife after each use, especially if it has been exposed to food, salt, or moisture.
- Apply a light food-safe oil to the blade pivot and hinge annually to ensure smooth operation.
- Sharpen the stainless steel blade with a fine ceramic or diamond sharpening stone. A sharp blade is safer than a dull one.
- Ensure the liner lock engages firmly each time you open the blade.

Sighting Compass:
- Keep the compass away from strong magnets, electronics, and speakers that can affect the magnetic needle.
- Store at room temperature. Extreme cold can cause the oil-filled capsule to develop bubbles (these typically disappear when the compass warms up).
- Clean the mirror and baseplate with a soft cloth. Avoid solvents.

Section 8: Climbing Gear Care

Climbing Harness (ConfortPro):
- Inspect the harness before every climb. Look for fraying, cuts, discoloration, or damage to the webbing, buckles, and belay loop.
- Wash by hand with lukewarm water and mild soap. Hang dry in the shade — UV light degrades nylon webbing.
- Retire a harness after 7–10 years of infrequent use, or after 3–5 years of regular use, even if it shows no visible damage. Nylon degrades over time regardless of visible condition.
- After any significant fall, inspect the harness carefully and consider retiring it.
- Our ConfortPro harness is certified to CE EN 12277. Never modify or alter a certified harness.

Climbing Rope (Dynamic 70m 9.5mm):
- Inspect the rope along its entire length before and after each use. Feel for soft spots, lumps, or a flat/stiff section that could indicate core damage.
- Wash by hand or in a front-loading machine on a gentle cycle with lukewarm water. Use a rope-specific cleaner or no cleaner at all. Hang dry in the shade.
- Flake and store loosely coiled in a cool, dry, dark place. Avoid storing on concrete floors (chemicals in concrete degrade nylon).
- Our rope has dry treatment for wet conditions, but the treatment will wear over time. Keep the rope away from acids, battery acid, bleach, and UV exposure.
- Track the number of lead falls and total use hours. Retire a rope immediately if the sheath is damaged enough to expose the core.

Section 9: Hydration Equipment Care

Insulated Water Bottle (1L Stainless Steel):
- Wash the bottle and cap with warm soapy water after each use. Use a bottle brush for thorough cleaning.
- For deep cleaning or odor removal, fill with a mixture of warm water and baking soda, let sit overnight, then rinse thoroughly.
- The 18/8 stainless steel construction is naturally resistant to odors and stains, but regular cleaning prevents bacterial buildup.
- The double-wall vacuum insulation maintains temperature (cold 24h, hot 12h) — do not put in the microwave or freezer.

Portable Water Filter (MiniPure):
- After each use, blow residual water out of the filter to prevent bacterial growth and freezing damage.
- If flow rate decreases, backflush the filter according to the manufacturer's instructions.
- Never allow the filter to freeze when wet, as ice crystals can damage the hollow fiber membrane and compromise filtration effectiveness.
- The MiniPure has a lifespan of 4,000 liters — track your usage and replace the filter element when approaching this limit.
- Store the filter in a clean, dry bag with the caps on.

General Tips for All Gear:
1. Inspect before every trip — catch small issues before they become big problems in the field.
2. Keep a repair kit in your pack — duct tape, tenacious tape, spare buckle, needle and thread.
3. Record purchase dates — most gear has recommended retirement dates based on age and use.
4. Avoid storing gear in garages, attics, or cars where temperature extremes and moisture can degrade materials.
5. When in doubt, contact Outlander Gear Co. customer support for specific care advice for any product in our catalog.
